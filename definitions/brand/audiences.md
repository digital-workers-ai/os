Three people. Write to one of them at a time, never to all three at once.

## The founder who became the analyst

Runs a company of 5 to 50 people. Pays for a dozen tools. Spends part of every
week pulling figures out of them by hand and reconciling the ones that
disagree. Has been told twice that a spreadsheet was wrong after a decision
was already made on it.

What they want: one number they can repeat in a board meeting without
checking it first.

What they fear: another subscription that adds a tab to look at.

Reach them with: the traceability, the nightly agent, the time back.

## The operator who owns the tools

Head of ops, or the first ops hire. Knows exactly where each tool's data is
wrong and why. Has already built two Zapier chains and a Google Sheet holding
the whole company together.

What they want: the mess written down somewhere that checks itself, so the
knowledge stops living in their head.

What they fear: a black box that redefines their numbers without telling them.

Reach them with: the definition files, the build checks that refuse to start
on definitions that disagree, the review queue for look-alike records.

## The technical buyer

A CTO, a lead engineer, or a founder who codes. Will read the repository
before the landing page. Will check the licence.

What they want: to see the architecture, the tests, and where the model is
and is not in the loop.

What they fear: AI writing numbers. Vendor lock-in.

Reach them with: the model gets finished numbers, never the database. Open
source, AGPL-3.0. The whole thing runs on Docker on their own machine.

## Not our audience

- Enterprises with a data team. They already solved this.
- Anyone who wants a chatbot over a warehouse. That is not what this is.
- Anyone shopping on price alone. Setup is work, and we say so.
