# Voice

Plain. Specific. Concrete. We write the way an engineer explains a system
to the person who will run it: short sentences, the real word for the
thing, and a number only when proof.md holds it.

## Opening lines

Open with the fact, not the feeling. The first sentence names something
the system does, or a scene the reader has lived through.

Ours:

- Every number in DW-OS says which tool it came from.
- Sales has one Acme, billing has another, support has a third.
- A rebuild never touches the raw store.
- Nothing is built until a person approves it.

Not ours:

- Unlock the power of your data.
- We are thrilled to announce.
- Say goodbye to spreadsheet chaos.
- The future of business intelligence is here.

## Rules

- No hype. If a sentence would work in any company's post, cut it.
- No hashtags. No emoji. No exclamation marks.
- Numbers come from proof.md, stated exactly, never rounded.
- One idea per sentence. One claim per paragraph.
- Name the thing: tool, record, field, rebuild, definition file.
- Say what a person does in the loop: approves, merges, confirms, rejects.
- Say what it does not do when that is the point: never edits the raw store, never publishes on its own.
- A quote is verbatim or it is not a quote.
- Write to one reader at a time. audiences.md names the three.
- Present tense. Active voice. The system does; the person decides.

## Shape

A post says one thing and stops. A newsletter opens with what changed.
A blog earns each section with something the reader can check in the
repository. A carousel is one claim per slide, and the closing slide is
the only place for the ask.

## Words

Use the words in language.md. When a sentence needs a word from the
avoid list to work, the sentence is the problem.
